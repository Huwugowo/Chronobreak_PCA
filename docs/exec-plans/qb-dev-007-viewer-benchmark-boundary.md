# QB-DEV-007 — Viewer benchmark execution boundary

## Purpose

Remove experiment orchestration and action execution from `ViewerScreen.tsx` while measuring the same real Viewer. Use its existing primary media element/controller as transport authority, preserving existing secondary clip-preview/background presentation, layout and clip behavior, explicit resource ownership and unchanged measurement contracts. This continues QB-DEV-004's bounded App extraction; it is not full frontend bundle isolation.

## Relevant planning-time architecture

`app/src/components/ViewerScreen.tsx:ViewerScreen` reads a token-bound descriptor and records opening attribution at its actual request/admission. Its `PlaybackSurface` owns the real `PlaybackController`/HTML video adapter, `ReplayDataController`, `ReplayOverlayController`, clip range/endpoints, fullscreen layout, decoder diagnostics, snapshot/frame subscriptions and one-second production diagnostics. Full details initialize after two animation frames; their admission remains independent of mounting the descriptor's native media.

Avoidable experiment state currently lives around lines 186–201, readiness/failure hooks at 230–285, event/evidence fan-out and seek at 414–457, the waits/action switch/scenario loop at 657–1127, and readiness timeout/cleanup at 1255–1290. These are planning-time locations, not stable line contracts. The action executor calls the same controller/helpers used by ordinary Viewer interactions. `seekTo` currently mixes production loop/preview updates with benchmark ID allocation, evidence listeners, dispatch notification and timeouts. Extraction must separate these concerns without bypassing loop-range/preview behavior.

`benchmarkApplication.ts` already owns App scenario/readiness/export policy and cross-screen cycles. The optional synchronous `onBenchmarkViewerCycleCompleted` callback captures the outgoing App context; the coordinator validates that identity before returning/reopening. Keep it unchanged. Cycle return renders real Games without the additional user `openTab` refresh. Backend export remains accurately scoped as `backend_command`, not exporter UI.

`benchmark.ts:buildScenarioActions` owns the existing action union and workload construction. `benchmarkSeekEvidence.ts` requires matching generation/action, dispatch plus native settlement and authoritative presentation in either arrival order (or explicit dedupe); replacement, recovery, disposal and failures reject. `benchmarkResize.ts` owns native resize policy/restoration, but currently accepts the entire controller and raw video. `benchmarkRender.ts` owns actual DOM/image decode plus paint readiness. `docs/architecture/desktop-replay.md` and the Viewer synchronization section of `docs/architecture/replay-time.md` define current production authorities. Requested preview, native seek completion and RVFC presentation remain distinct.

## Scope and non-goals

Move the Viewer action switch, scenario eligibility/completion policy, benchmark-only waits/deadlines/RAF/abort/evidence maps, endpoint alternation and terminal handling into a harness-owned Viewer runner. Narrow resize/render helper inputs where necessary to avoid exposing a full controller or raw video. Add a short owner-bound adapter in the real screen.

Do not change playback algorithms, recovery, media/data admission, UI behavior, clip heuristics/frame conversions, event schemas/filtering, scenario definitions, observer overhead contracts, analyzer thresholds, Rust activation or export staging/recovery. Do not add benchmark-owned media/controller instances, a generic bus, global runner, service registry, benchmark-only data projection or fake ready state; existing secondary preview media stays under its current production owner. Production observation markers, real search/history seams, decoder diagnostics, historical duration compatibility, App no-refresh cycle behavior and frontend bundle packaging remain justified separate scope. No dynamic import/build mode or claim of harness bytes being absent from normal artifacts.

## Exploration findings

- The scenario switch is one coherent migration; moving arbitrary screen closures wholesale would relocate coupling. The host must expose concrete operations/read-only observations, not component setters/controllers or another scenario interpreter.
- Controller event delivery is synchronous and can happen inside a seek call. Evidence and dispatch listeners must be registered before requesting the seek. A new subscription after dispatch would miss valid evidence.
- Non-opening scenarios require details plus ready media and current-generation canplay/first presented frame. Versioned opening starts media actions before full details, but cannot succeed until full detail admission. Changing either gate changes measured opening work.
- Success currently pauses, refreshes/samples metrics, captures the completion payload, releases the controller, waits 100 ms, emits completion, marks finished, then synchronously calls App or finalizes the terminal. The callback can immediately unmount the Viewer. Unexpected disposal before that boundary must remain a failure.
- Resize/render have real ownership obligations: resize drains an already-issued request and restores geometry even after abort; render checks actual images and source/tick identity. Cancelling a promise does not undo native window work.
- Existing Viewer tests mock workload construction and resize. They establish wiring/readiness/seek/callback behavior but do not freeze every action's ordered runner trace. Add such coverage before structural edits.

## Chosen design and rationale

### Runner lifetime and observation binding

Add `app/src/benchmarkViewer.ts`, exporting a typed `createBenchmarkViewer(observer, host, context)` and its handle. `context` captures the existing opening metadata (when applicable), game/media identity and a private Viewer mount token. The handle exposes focused synchronous notifications (`onPlaybackEvent`, `detailsAdmitted`, `detailsFailed`, `updateReadiness`, `dispose`) rather than an externally callable action executor. The runner owns scenario selection/building, one-start/one-finish latches, readiness generation evidence, details-admission promise, 30,000 ms readiness deadline, 6,000 ms seek/action timeout policy, abortable waits/frames, pending evidence/dispatch observers and endpoint sequence.

Create it synchronously in the `PlaybackSurface` mount owner after the actual controller exists and before `controller.open` can emit events. Register cleanup synchronously in that same owner. Capture the nullable observer once for the binding; a no-session Viewer creates no runner, benchmark abort controller, readiness timer, evidence map or benchmark frame loop. Guard delayed callbacks with the captured observer identity and mount token. Session replacement/disposal cannot credit an outgoing Viewer to a new observer. No asynchronous callback acquires implicit Solid cleanup ownership.

The creation path must not miss details already admitted before runner construction: initialize from live state, then forward real admissions. Notifications may repeat; starting stays idempotent. Feed ordinary reactive readiness through a separate focused effect; it must not recreate the runner. Call an explicit `armReadinessDeadline()` notification at the current end-of-mount timer-arm boundary, after the real open attempt and keyboard/metrics setup, not at descriptor request or runner construction.

Keep controller event emission at `onPlaybackEvent`, preserving payload/opening/generation/action identity and its synchronous order. Immediately after that emission, forward the original event to the captured runner, which fans it out to its pending evidence/dispatch waiters and updates canplay/first-frame generation latches. Forwarding is observation, not a new event source. Preserve the current queued-microtask eligibility boundary after readiness events/details; never start actions reentrantly from a controller event or reactive effect.

Keep descriptor/opening-media, detail payload, decoder, fullscreen-applied and controller/frame instrumentation at their real sources. A pure harness helper may select the existing opening context so the screen need not interpret scenario kinds. Descriptor failures before `PlaybackSurface` exists remain a small observation/failure adapter with the same phase and terminal payload; do not instantiate a fake media runner to report them. Detail admission must retain `opening_details_ready` -> admission notification/deadline reconciliation -> `playback_payload_ready` -> queued eligibility ordering. The runner receives already-admitted facts and errors; it never calls data controllers or synthesizes their markers.

### Bounded host and shared production operations

Define the host type next to the runner. Read operations return existing immutable domain types or focused `Pick`/readonly projections; they must read live objects when called. Required capabilities are:

| Capability | Production owner and contract |
| --- | --- |
| Read playback, duration/end tick, details status and presentation clock | Actual controller snapshot and current admitted Viewer state. No cached replacement state or wall-clock-derived media position. |
| Play, pause, set rate | Existing controller operations and their real outcomes. Runner owns workload intent/failure telemetry. Ordinary user callbacks keep their present failure behavior. |
| Request a seek with target/reason/playAfter/optional action ID | One shared production primitive applies current clip loop range, calls `controller.seek`, updates requested preview and returns the actual `SeekOutcome`. Both UI and runner use it. |
| Prepare event jump / edit clip endpoint / set or cancel clip selection | Existing nearest-event, anchor, frame-aligned endpoint and editing helpers over real clip state. Return the chosen target/range/event facts needed for unchanged telemetry; no benchmark counters or reason selection in the host. |
| Set fullscreen with required-observation intent | Existing enter/exit functions and real layout effect. Keep the required bit until the actual applied event; runner does not synthesize applied layout. |
| Refresh server/player metrics and sample quality | Existing `updateMetrics`/`sampleMetrics` at the same points and count; return real captured completion fields/server metrics. |
| Release media and notify completed cycle | Idempotent disposal of the actual controller; existing synchronous captured App callback. The runner owns the 100 ms wait and completion decision. |
| Resize/render observation subjects | Narrow read-only live identity/connection/source observations, frame subscription and the real overlay root/rendered-state read described below. No controller/video ownership transfer. |

Factor only the production seek primitive needed by both paths; keep UI `seekTo` as a thin wrapper preserving existing ordinary benchmark-session action-ID allocation and outcome/error behavior. The harness allocates exactly one action ID for each workload seek and supplies it to that primitive, avoiding double allocation. Before calling it, register `createBenchmarkSeekEvidence` and any layout dispatch waiter in runner-owned maps; remove them on settle, timeout, replacement and disposal. Forward all relevant original controller events, including user seeks, so a newer UI intent still supersedes pending workload evidence. Preserve final-scrub evidence, intermediate-request semantics, dual completion ordering, dedupe and rejected/superseded outcomes. Remove `onBenchmarkDispatch`/`awaitBenchmarkEvidence` and benchmark timeout policy from the production seek primitive.

Endpoint alternation belongs to the runner; production operations accept the explicit endpoint, apply existing frame math and return the preview target/range. A focused `finishClipEndpointEdit()` capability clears editing state at the existing successful-seek boundary before the 100 ms wait; do not expose a general signal setter. Event-jump selection and clip-mode operations reuse the current Viewer helpers/data. Do not copy roster/projection or clip algorithms into the runner. Ordinary controls and benchmark actions must share these transformations and the same seek primitive.

For resize, narrow `runBenchmarkDesktopResize` to a typed subject with only `snapshot`, `play`, `pause`, `subscribeFrames` and a synchronous `readMediaIdentity()` returning the actual mounted element's connection/currentSrc identity (and an opaque element token). Its native window dependency remains in the harness helper. The screen binds those methods to its one existing controller/video; the helper's identity checks, request/event/frame timing, 8+8 loop, restoration and deadlines stay unchanged. Do not put a `runDesktopResize` experiment implementation back into the screen or expose a whole controller/video merely for convenience.

For render, define a narrow read-only subject with `root(): Element | null` for the actual fullscreen overlay and `read(): RenderSubjectState`. Its exact state fields are: `elementToken: object` (opaque stable identity of the mounted primary, never the element itself), `connected: boolean`, `assetsAvailable: boolean`, `generation: number`, `sessionToken: string | null`, `sourceUrl: string | null`, `recoveryCount: number`, `paused: boolean`, `activeSource`, `overlaySource`, `windowSource` (each `Readonly<SourceEpoch> | null` from `leagueSource.ts`), `presentedTick: ReplayTick | null`, and `rendered: BenchmarkRenderedState | null`. The latter uses existing production participant/overlay projection helpers and actual asset base; missing sample/window remains null, never fabricated data. These fields supply the exact current checks without exposing data-controller ownership.

At action entry require a primary/overlay root and available assets, otherwise preserve the immediate `render readiness surface/assets unavailable` failure; require an active source, otherwise preserve `render readiness source unavailable`. Then capture element/root/load identity and active source. Every read and the post-decode/two-RAF check compares against that captured identity: same connected element/root, generation, session token, source URL, recovery count, paused media and active source. Active/overlay/window sources must agree before admission; subsequently null/missing facts keep waiting within the existing deadline, while changed established identity fails. Require a presented 52,433 ms within one frame, ten participants and real events. After decode/paint also preserve the helper's existing generation/source-token/tick/image-array identity checks. The runner/helper owns these comparisons and the 52,433 ms experiment precondition; the screen only supplies live facts. Keep image selection/decode/DOM identity/two-frame logic in `benchmarkRender.ts`. A real read-only overlay root is a justified observation seam, not permission for harness DOM mutation.

### Completion, cancellation and failure

Preserve the separate ordinary/versioned-opening gates and the existing readiness deadline clearing rules. Non-opening start clears its media timer; opening retains the deadline until started plus details-ready, and after its actions waits for admitted details with the existing 30-second timeout. Keep current failure reasons/phases (`replay_descriptor`, `league_replay_window`, `media_readiness`, `actions`, `viewer_disposed`) and action-failure ordering. Do not replace current successful terminals with fallback completion.

On success, preserve pause -> metrics refresh -> sample/capture completion payload -> controller release -> 100 ms -> required scenario/cycle event -> finished latch -> synchronous callback or terminal. The intentional release phase remains eligible for this completion even though controller readiness has become disposed; mount/session guards must not mistake it for unexpected unmount. Mark finished before the callback because App may synchronously unmount. With no pending successful cycle, active unexpected unmount retains its explicit failed terminal; stale/replaced sessions do not publish into the new session. Opening failure aborts and releases immediately as today; ordinary action failure emits the required failure and follows its existing release/terminal path. Use one terminal latch per runner and the existing observer's completion authority.

`dispose()` first makes scheduled continuations ineligible, settles the applicable unexpected-disposal failure once, aborts waits, clears its readiness/timer/RAF resources and evidence maps, and releases runner-owned subscriptions. The screen still owns controller/data/diagnostic/keyboard/metrics cleanup and may dispose the controller again safely. Native resize cleanup is a bounded cleanup obligation allowed after publication eligibility ends: drain any issued request, restore geometry, unlisten exactly once. Check cancellation before every later workload mutation/emission and after asynchronous boundaries, without suppressing that restoration. Do not add a universal generation-equality guard that rejects intentional controller recovery before its real failure evidence is observed; action-specific identity contracts remain authoritative.

If baseline contract tests expose an independent defect, retain its failure and record separate canonical work before changing semantics. Do not silently repair unrelated behavior during extraction or weaken the frozen expectations.

## Rejected alternatives and planning decisions

- A runner accepting all screen setters/controller/video would preserve the coupling under a new filename. Use the bounded live host and shared production primitive.
- A second player, direct `currentTime` workload writes or alternate replay/data queries would change the measured product. Retain the real controller, frame evidence and admitted projections.
- Moving all instrumentation into the runner would risk turning workload intent into observed readiness. Retain real-source emission and explicitly forward evidence.
- Leave App cycle/navigation/export policy where QB-DEV-004 put it. Do not combine another App migration, duration rewrite, frontend packaging change or global bus with this unit.
- Retain current measurement sampling/order; do not use this refactor to simplify away the opening/details split, media-release delay, required fullscreen bit or resize restoration.
