# QB-AUDIO-003 — Native system loopback with independent audio failure

## Purpose

Capture ordinary Windows playback as one synchronized system-audio track while protecting gameplay video. **Audio acquisition failures change audio availability and quality; they do not invalidate independently healthy video.** A genuine video capture, encoder, shared mux, storage or invalid-container failure still follows the existing recording failure path.

This design supersedes the immutable v1 and v2 plans following the user's final engineering review. It retains native WASAPI, independent audio degradation, the existing video pipeline and strict publication. It makes producer lead, startup pre-roll, final tail and the shared-FFmpeg failure boundary explicit. Preserve both predecessor plans as history.

## Relevant planning-time architecture

- `recorder/src/encoder.rs` owns `AudioSource`, `detect_audio_source`, device-name matching and Windows audio arguments. Windows currently lists DirectShow capture devices once at service startup and falls back to `anullsrc`; this is not endpoint loopback.
- `recorder/src/native/session.rs` obtains the first real WGC QPC epoch before starting the mux/NVENC graph. The exact-CFR video clock, GPU conversion, NVENC admission/completion and frame reconciliation already work independently of audio acquisition.
- `recorder/src/native/mux.rs` starts one locked FFmpeg child: native Annex-B H.264 on stdin, selected audio as another input, video stream copy with the existing ordinal-to-CFR `setts` filter, AAC and fragmented MP4. Audio-first opening exists. The current `-shortest` and mandatory audio mapping need explicit finite/absent-audio variants. `validate_mux_only` currently forbids probe overrides as well as video capture/filter/encode work.
- Native startup readiness and the service watchdog use real WGC/encode/mux progress under existing deadlines. `RecordingEvidence.protocol_error` is fatal; audio-local outcomes must not enter it. Native finish currently drains NVENC, finishes FFmpeg, then closes WGC.
- `service.rs` always supplies `expected_audio_codec: Some("aac")` to the common finalizer. In `replay-time/src/lib.rs`, `None` means audio must be absent, not optional. Introduce a distinct optional-AAC expectation; do not reinterpret `None` or relax the video checks. `MediaTimelineV2` already represents no audio and independent finite audio coverage, capped to video for replay/clip authority.
- Recorder `CaptureMetadata` in `poller.rs` and the app's strict `CaptureMetadataDocument` in `app/src-tauri/src/library.rs` must both know any additive audio summary. The existing recorder-control `CaptureError` means the recording failed, so it is unsuitable for audio degradation. This repair reports through bounded warning logs, diagnostics and saved metadata without adding a new UI lifecycle.

Use the existing `windows` 0.62 bindings and exact packaged `MediaTools` pair. No runtime rebuild or alternative tool discovery is needed. Current generated native fixtures use intentional silence; existing TCP PCM/mux fixtures prove neither endpoint capture nor named-pipe behavior.

## Scope and non-goals

One default `eRender/eConsole` endpoint, fixed 48-kHz stereo PCM, one AAC track when available, one recording identity and one FFmpeg mux child. Preserve WGC → D3D11 → NVENC, video timestamps, fragmented MP4, strict validation and no-replace publication.

Separate League/everything-else tracks, process-loopback inclusion/exclusion, multiple endpoints, microphone audio, new replay/export controls, alternate video backends, post-recording audio remux and a custom resampler/PLL are outside this feature. Existing `silent/none` configuration remains explicit intentional silence. Non-Windows capture behavior stays unchanged.

## Exploration findings and principal risks

Microsoft's [endpoint loopback API](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/coreaudio/loopback-recording) captures a shared-mode render endpoint without requiring Stereo Mix. Event-driven loopback is supported on Chronobreak's Windows baseline. [GetBuffer](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/audioclient/nf-audioclient-iaudiocaptureclient-getbuffer) provides a first-sample QPC value in 100-ns units; an empty buffer supplies no new sample/timestamp evidence. Neither pipe arrival time nor stream existence proves capture or synchronization.

The main integration risk is FFmpeg input opening/probing, not the Windows capture call. An audio-first pipe whose producer waits for video from its first sample could create a circular wait. A finite bootstrap allowance plus an audio-only EOF escape breaks that dependency; steady lead is bounded against existing mux progress. FFmpeg also documents a ten-second default interleave allowance; an explicit smaller, nonzero limit is needed to bound starvation buffering. Zero does not disable that buffering. See [FFmpeg format options](https://www.ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-formats.html).

The focused planning experiment used the hash-verified r6 runtime, a real Windows byte-mode named pipe, generated 360-frame H.264 input and the production video timestamp/fragment arguments. Closing with zero PCM produced an AAC-labelled track with zero packets, despite successful exit and misleading positive ffprobe duration. A four-byte half-stereo-frame prefix caused exit 69 and only one output video frame. Conversely, complete PCM prefixes (including a one-frame prefix and a 200-ms probing prefix), closure after observed AAC mux output, and tested server disconnect after a valid prefix retained all 360 exact-CFR video frames. A 500-ms cap against `-progress frame`, delayed video start and stalled video with an audio-only EOF escape also retained the full video. No probe overrides were needed.

These are transport facts, not WASAPI, NVENC, production-finalizer, memory-budget or negligible-overhead evidence. The short low-resolution fixture cannot prove every OS pipe failure, codec failure or GPU startup combination. In particular, a torn input can damage the shared mux: the guarantee covers independently failing endpoint acquisition and deliberately managed complete PCM/EOF, not arbitrary corruption of the shared FFmpeg input. The compact evidence at `docs/performance/results/qb-audio-003-transport.json` retains tested cases, exact arguments, adverse results and harness limitations. Raw outputs and the small one-off driver remain under its recorded ignored `build/perf` paths; do not turn this into a new framework.

Endpoint quietness, disconnection and timestamp anomalies are normal reliability concerns for a game recorder. Coverage uncertainty must remain visible without becoming a video failure. A short real-device quiet-to-sound test is necessary; deterministic timestamp tests cannot establish actual endpoint behavior.

## Chosen design and rationale

### 1. Separate video validity from audio outcome

| Condition | Audio action | Recording action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Valid system packets | Timestamp and feed PCM/AAC | Publish strictly validated video and present audio |
| Healthy client with no packets | Supply inferred idle silence; retain uncertain coverage | Continue video; empty buffers alone are not a fault |
| No endpoint, stopped audio service or failed capture initialization | Mark unavailable; run bounded audio recovery while the PCM pump supplies counted silence | Continue video without waiting for endpoint readiness |
| Device change or invalidation | Replace only the endpoint client; fill/account for the missing interval | Keep the epoch, video graph, mux and media identity |
| Bad timestamps, capture queue loss or audio worker failure | Discard unusable samples, record degradation; recover or disable capture | Continue video with counted fill or finite audio |
| Dedicated audio transport setup/open fails before video production | Select video-only; if necessary reap the initial child and restart once without audio | Start video within the same startup deadline |
| Managed audio EOF after a complete PCM prefix | Finish any partial sample write, close the writer handle and retain finite AAC | Continue video; validate actual output before publication |
| Broken/torn transport or shared audio encoder error | Stop audio writes, retain the error; inspect existing child/video health | Video survival is conditional, never guaranteed for a failing shared child or malformed media |
| Shared FFmpeg exit/failure, video failure, storage failure or invalid MP4 | Existing terminal handling | Preserve partial output; do not claim canonical success |

The normal transport stays open through recoverable endpoint outages, avoiding mux restarts. Silence generated for unavailable/uncertain capture is a delivery fallback, never evidence that sound was captured. Endpoint failure never closes or cancels an in-flight PCM write: the independent pump keeps delivering counted fill. Audio recovery expiry, invalid QPC and failed capture-owner cleanup do not become `CaptureError`, process-generation capture suppression, or a fatal `protocol_error`. Preserve actual shared mux failures and video watchdogs; do not suppress them under an audio label.

### 2. One capture owner and a small PCM timeline pump

Add a Windows native audio module under `recorder/src/native/`, with small checked timing and summary types that can be tested independently. One dedicated MTA COM owner creates, uses and releases the enumerator, client and capture service. Select through `IMMDeviceEnumerator::GetDefaultAudioEndpoint(eRender, eConsole)`, initialize `IAudioClient` with shared-mode `LOOPBACK | EVENTCALLBACK | AUTOCONVERTPCM`, set its event and obtain `IAudioCaptureClient`. Use explicit float32 48-kHz stereo and the normal shared engine period. Windows supplies the required matrix/conversion; [conversion flags](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/coreaudio/audclnt-streamflags-xxx-constants) document the higher-cost default-quality SRC option. Select its use based on the short audible conversion check, without adding a second native resampling pipeline.

Drain available packets on capture events; copy into reusable bounded storage and promptly release buffers on that same thread. The [Rust binding](https://microsoft.github.io/windows-docs-rs/doc/windows/Win32/Media/Audio/struct.IAudioCaptureClient.html) is not `Send`/`Sync`. Keep COM pointers on their owner and use ordinary owned PCM/control data across the boundary. Do not introduce unsafe thread-safety wrappers.

Start this capture owner asynchronously immediately after recording admission, before constructing WGC or awaiting its first frame. Thread creation returns immediately; COM activation, format negotiation and endpoint readiness are never awaited by video initialization. Retain up to one second of timestamped pre-roll (384,000 PCM bytes at this format), within the combined 8 MiB budget. Overwrite the oldest pre-epoch blocks when full and count it. On epoch publication, trim samples before zero and transfer retained nonnegative spans by ownership; overwritten spans that overlap the eventual video timeline become startup coverage loss. Count ordinary pre-epoch trimming separately from that loss. Slow/failed audio activation still permits video start and explicit missing coverage. After epoch handoff, the total queued PCM bound is 500 ms; startup cannot silently grow it.

Use a separate pump/writer thread because named-pipe backpressure must not delay packet release or device callbacks. It handles timestamp placement, fill and cancellable pipe I/O. This justifies at most two new threads; do not build a general audio executor. Audio callbacks and the existing GPU worker perform no audio processing or pipe I/O.

Bound all application-owned audio buffers, queue capacities, scratch and in-flight copies to 8 MiB combined, and steady queued PCM to 500 ms. Copy each WASAPI packet once into a reusable owned block; pass block ownership and slice offsets, not cloned sample vectors. Trim by offsets and write borrowed slices directly where possible. Use one reusable zero block and bounded scratch only where unavoidable. Keep blocks alive until overlapped I/O completes. Wake the pump on packet/control/epoch/progress events, stop or I/O completion. Coalesce silence and ready samples into up-to-50-ms writes; arm a one-shot timer only for the next due fill/write or actual deadline. When capped, wait for mux progress or its escape deadline; when no epoch exists, wait for the epoch/control signal. No permanent 10-ms polling timer, wake-for-empty-queue loop, per-packet allocation or per-packet production logging. Queue overflow discards stale audio with explicit accounting; it cannot block video or grow storage. OS pipe buffers and FFmpeg memory are measured separately rather than hidden inside this application-memory claim.

### 3. Timestamp alignment without a custom clock controller

Share the existing first-WGC epoch as soon as it is established. Convert a valid packet anchor to a signed sample slot with checked rational arithmetic:

`sample_slot = nearest_ties_even((packet_qpc_100ns - wgc_epoch_100ns) * 48_000 / 10_000_000)`.

Trim samples before zero. Anchor a contiguous packet run using QPC, then retain its actual sample sequence and use device-position/sample-count continuity to detect gaps or overlap. Re-anchor after a real discontinuity, endpoint replacement or an idle interval already filled by the pump. Insert counted silence for a gap and trim overlapping/late data against the irrevocably emitted cursor. A resumed packet uses its valid QPC, never a fresh audio zero. Honor `SILENT` without dereferencing nonexistent sample data. A flagged invalid timestamp, impossible range or backward/inconsistent clock is an audio anomaly: discard the unusable packet and recover/degrade.

Do not re-edit every contiguous packet to chase QPC jitter. Record a compact maximum/last QPC-versus-sample residual so real drift is observable. No one-second estimator, ppm budget, interpolated sample slipping, adaptive rate loop or mandatory `IAudioClock` watermark belongs in this repair. If the real A/V proof reveals material drift, first establish its cause and propose a standard resampling correction as a separate design decision; do not invent a controller to make a test pass.

The pump uses the exact QPC/`Instant` anchor pair shared with the native video clock. A delivery allowance D postpones irreversible fill without moving media PTS. Select the shipped fixed default D during implementation from the required short real-device tone/quiet observation: measure last-sample QPC-to-drain delay, round the observed maximum up to a 50-ms block and add one block, with a 50–250 ms working range. Do not run a calibration benchmark at each recording startup. This is a measured tradeoff, never a packet-arrival guarantee; persist the selected D and observed maximum. If the observation exceeds that range or repeatedly loses valid sound, investigate delivery/ownership before declaring audio acceptance. Do not add adaptive buffering or a clock controller.

At each due range, prefer already received valid samples. Missing samples become `inferred_idle_fill`, `startup_missing_fill` or known `unavailable_fill`; WASAPI `SILENT` is separately counted confirmed silence. Retain a bounded recent interval ledger within the PCM storage budget to match late packets against generated fill. Count all valid frames discarded behind the emitted cursor as `late_valid_frames`; separately count non-silent-flagged frames intersecting prior inferred fill as `late_signal_coverage_frames` (potential audible loss, not proof of sound energy). Also retain late-packet count and maximum lateness. Any such lost valid coverage makes the outcome degraded, with one transition warning and a final count; repeated late arrivals cannot remain a captured-success claim. Very old arrivals beyond the bounded ledger retain total late-valid counts and an unknown attribution, rather than inventing precise overlap. No packet absence alone is evidence of malfunction or confirmed silence.

### 4. Bounded lead, transport and final tail

Keep H.264 on stdin and use one randomly named current-user-only local Windows byte-mode named pipe for explicit `f32le -ar 48000 -ac 2`. Create the server before spawning FFmpeg; reject remote clients and enforce first-instance protection. Use overlapped cancellable connection/writes with owned buffers. Preserve explicit maps, `-c:v copy`, the existing exact video `setts`, negative AAC priming semantics and fragment/flush flags. Remove `-shortest`. Video-only omits audio input/map/encoder arguments entirely; optional mapping cannot repair a failed input.

Define these sample-coordinate quantities at 48 kHz / 60 FPS:

- `M`: monotonically observed FFmpeg `-progress frame`, capped/checked against video producer accounting; its end is `800 * M` samples. It is delayed evidence of consumed video, not NVENC admission or an audio clock. Do not use `completed_frames`, which can increment even after an output-write error, or bytes merely accepted by a `BufWriter`.
- `A`: cumulative complete PCM frames sent, plus the full reserved length of the one in-flight write. Reserving before dispatch prevents a partial write or stop race from exceeding the bound.
- `B = L = 24,000` frames (500 ms): bootstrap allowance and maximum audio lead. For every write, require `A <= max(B, 800*M + L)`. Use checked integer arithmetic. No positive M is needed for the initial B.
- The normal time-based due cursor also limits writes to elapsed epoch time minus D. The lead cap only restricts that cursor; it never invents timestamps or authorizes unpaced unlimited bootstrap.

Reuse the mux's existing 250-ms progress cadence and publish/wake once per changed progress snapshot. Video, its callbacks, NVENC submission and the completion writer never wait on this audio cap. A capped pump that receives no increase of M for one second closes audio at a complete-frame boundary and records `audio_lead_stall_eof`; this releases possible input/probe starvation while video continues. Apply the escape during startup and steady operation. It does not reset the mux, relax the cap or extend the video watchdog. An audio write itself has a one-second deadline; endpoint failures do not cancel it. If cancellation leaves an incomplete stereo frame and completing that frame cannot succeed within the deadline, classify the transport as uncertain/shared-risk, not a proven safe EOF. Observing a valid prefix is not proof every later corrupted write is harmless.

Keep 32-KiB pipe buffers, an explicit 32-packet input `thread_queue_size` for each input, and nonzero `max_interleave_delta=250000`. Those options were exercised together with the lead cap. They bound configured queues; they do not prove an all-process memory cap, maximum demux packet size, hard latency or total FFmpeg heap bound. Short integration resource observations must check FFmpeg separately from the 8 MiB application budget. Keep the current bans on probe overrides and all video work: the focused experiment required no `probesize`, `analyzeduration`, `fpsprobesize` or `nobuffer` changes.

**Startup:** after epoch publication, connect and send a complete initial 4,096-frame PCM prefix (85.33 ms, 32 KiB) as one overlapped write within the 500-ms allowance. This is the sole exception to ordinary up-to-50-ms writes; one bounded startup scratch block may assemble retained spans and fill. The focused experiment found that two smaller writes while withholding video could stall the handshake; one complete 32-KiB write succeeded before video started. Use retained real pre-roll when available, and explicitly counted fill only when its D-based due time arrives. Give the handshake at most one second within the original 15-second startup deadline. A partial write does not satisfy it. Until this succeeds, do not start video production in that child. On setup/connect/initial-write failure, terminate and reap the not-yet-video-producing child and choose video-only once, within the same absolute deadline. Retain WGC and its original epoch; never restart a recording mux after video has begun. This initial PCM prefix does not assert AAC has already been encoded; the experiment establishes that a complete prefix followed by managed EOF can be finalized. Do not wait for AAC output or completed probing before starting video.

**During capture:** endpoint invalidation, bad timestamps and capture-owner failure affect the producer/summary only. The pump owns the pipe independently and substitutes accountable fill, so those failures do not become input corruption or EOF. Managed EOF remains the bounded escape for a capped/stalled pump and orderly stop. Unexpected pipe break, cancelled partial I/O or FFmpeg AAC failure belongs to the shared transport boundary: keep video running only while existing video/mux health permits, then validate the actual candidate. No claim that any audio-input failure is universally isolated inside one process.

**Stop:** freeze final accepted video count `Vf` when tick admission closes, signal it to the pump before encoder drain, and cancel capture/retries independently. Stop scheduling writes beyond `800*Vf`; existing in-flight reservations are already counted in A. The stop deadline is 500 ms from this signal and supersedes the ordinary one-second write/lead wait, including any pending I/O. Drain available audio/fill to that boundary only within this window and the lead cap. Do not wait for new endpoint packets or require a clock proof. At expiry cancel pending I/O; observe cancellation before freeing its buffer/event and close the pipe. Finish a partial stereo frame only if it can be done within the same deadline; otherwise record uncertain transport/shared risk, not clean EOF. OS cancellation acknowledgement is cooperative, not a hard kernel-call guarantee; any outstanding owner retains its I/O resources independently of GPU/video state. Capture-owner cleanup is never a prerequisite to video drain. If the boundary cannot be reached, record a short audio tail. Audio EOF and video drain proceed concurrently; both inputs close before the existing mux reap. Normal full-frame writes imply an irreversible raw-PCM overhang of at most L (500 ms) even on a very short recording. Do not add extra tail padding after the boundary. AAC coded priming/padding is separate: validate and report actual coverage, with an integration upper bound of L plus 2 AAC frames (2,048 samples, about 542.7 ms total positive overhang). The observed runtime used one extra 1,024-sample frame; two is a finite acceptance allowance, not permission to shift video zero. Replay/clip duration stays video-authoritative. A shared transport failure may leave shorter/invalid media and remains subject to strict rejection.

### 5. Recover one endpoint safely

Resolve the default endpoint per recording, rather than selecting a DirectShow device at daemon startup. `silent/none` stays intentional silence; unset/default/system requests WASAPI. Reject obsolete named-DirectShow overrides with a migration message instead of quietly remapping them. An unavailable requested system source is a nonfatal audio outcome.

Use one coalesced desired-endpoint generation and one retry scheduler. Relevant default-render-role changes, current-device state/format changes or API invalidation cause release/reacquisition of only the audio client, following [Microsoft's recovery sequence](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/coreaudio/recovering-from-an-invalid-device-error). Reread the default after registering/opening so a stale open cannot replace a newer endpoint. Device sample positions restart per client; the recording epoch never does.

Attempt promptly and no faster than once per second during a five-second initial recovery window. After that, report unavailable and use a low-frequency retry, initially every 30 seconds, until stop or a relevant device notification. Coalesce notification storms and rate-limit retries; notifications do not extend a fast-retry window indefinitely. These limits bound audio recovery work and status changes, never video survival. Quiet clients can use a low-frequency health check for service/invalidation errors; packet absence alone is not an error. No healthy-state periodic device enumeration is needed.

Notification callbacks only update bounded state and signal the owner. Microsoft's [callback rules](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/mmdeviceapi/nn-mmdeviceapi-immnotificationclient) prohibit blocking/reentrant unregister. The owner retains a strong callback reference through [unregistration](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/mmdeviceapi/nf-mmdeviceapi-immdeviceenumerator-registerendpointnotificationcallback). If safe unregister/release cannot be established, retain only that independent registration owner/callback/event state, bounded to one retained owner for the process, and disable subsequent audio capture until relaunch. The retained state owns no GPU, video pipe, mux or recording output. This precise lifetime safeguard is not a general quarantine subsystem and does not disable subsequent video recordings.

Capture-owner cancellation, panic and teardown errors are reported separately from transport integrity. Ordinary cleanup is owned and joined with finite waits; existing cooperative Windows-call limitations are not a hard driver-hang guarantee. Audio state must be independently owned so a stalled audio owner cannot hold video finalization hostage. If an owner must outlive its cancellation wait, the same single-owner retention bound applies and new audio is disabled; the pump can still close independently. Do not free potentially live callback state to meet a timeout.

### 6. Strict publication and compact truthful evidence

Replace the finalizer's ambiguous `Option<String>` with one explicit `AudioExpectation` enum: `RequiredCodec(codec)`, `OptionalCodec(codec)` and `Absent`. Required accepts exactly one valid matching stream; optional accepts no audio or one valid matching stream; absent accepts no audio. Extra streams and malformed present streams fail in every mode. Windows system capture uses optional AAC; intentional-silence and existing callers retain their deliberate expectations. Present audio still needs valid codec, sample rate, time base, nonempty sample evidence and coverage; malformed audio/container data is not relabeled as harmless capture degradation. Exact nonempty video grid/count, mux success, zero video origin, bounded common probing and no-replace publication remain mandatory. Do not change the persisted `MediaTimelineV2` schema. An AAC-labelled empty track must fail present-audio validation even when ffprobe synthesizes positive duration: extend the existing bounded fragment inspection to require a nonzero sample run for the present audio track, without a full decode or another production probe pass.

Update all expectation constructors, including `recorder/src/finalizer/recovery.rs`. Recovery already has a staged, fully validated terminal timeline and requires exact equality on re-probe. Preserve that stronger contract: reconstruct `RequiredCodec` for its recorded present audio and `Absent` for its recorded absent audio, never optional based on an unvalidated probe or `capture.audio` status. Do not add a second persisted expected-media authority or let optional capture erase a previously validated track during recovery. Add a focused generated pending-publication regression for video-only and finite AAC outcomes; existing receipt, generation and no-overwrite protections remain unchanged.

Keep outer `CaptureMetadata.schema_version: 1` and add `audio: Option<AudioCaptureSummaryV1>` with its own nested version 1 in both recorder and strict app metadata types. Keep source mode, outcome (captured, idle/uncertain, degraded, unavailable or intentional silence), actual input/output counts, fill counts by reason, discarded frames/discontinuities, reopen count, first/last valid QPC, chosen delivery allowance/maximum observed delay, late-valid/late-signal-coverage counts, inferred versus confirmed silence, lead/tail maxima and compact residual/last-error summary. Reconcile the outcome with the validated final stream: video-only, fallback-only AAC, finite captured AAC and captured AAC are distinct facts. A retained finite AAC prefix remains valid media but has a degraded capture outcome. All-fallback output cannot claim successful native sound capture. Missing legacy summaries remain unknown. Preserve the summary unchanged during app save and reject unknown nested fields/versions consistently, including staged recovery metadata; it is provenance, not another media authority.

Emit one warning on loss/degradation and a recovery/final summary, without per-packet logs. Diagnostics distinguish packets observed, activated-but-idle/uncertain, intentional silence and unavailable capture. Do not use a present AAC track or successful activation as the audible proof. UI presentation of that saved status can be added separately; do not repurpose the existing fatal capture-error state.

## Rejected alternatives and planning decisions

- More DirectShow name matching cannot provide ordinary endpoint loopback reliably.
- Perfect audio or failed recording contradicts the user's explicit video-preservation requirement.
- A new audio clock authority, idle watermark proof and custom drift controller remain unnecessary. A coarse one-way lead cap with independent bootstrap and an EOF escape bounds irreversible PCM without making video wait for audio.
- Keep PCM healthy through endpoint outages. Restarting FFmpeg is permitted only once before video production for failed initial setup. After video begins, arbitrary transport corruption is a shared-mux risk; managed complete-frame EOF has its separately demonstrated limited guarantee.
- A second encoder process, native AAC stack, timestamped multimedia transport or alternate video backend is unnecessary for one track. The early proof is the boundary for challenging the chosen raw-PCM transport.

Use generated tones/windows and sentinel-owned temporary outputs only. Reuse existing fixture tools; no new benchmark or verification framework.

| A/V alignment | One short actual WGC/NVENC recording with early and late flash/tone markers. Compare decoded media markers with observed WGC/render QPC, not enqueue times or separately zeroed tracks. Require at most 20 ms marker mapping error and at most 10 ms early-to-late change. ## Performance and reliability gates

One live shared FFmpeg child, with at most one video-only retry before video production; 500-ms audio lead including in-flight writes and bootstrap; bounded 500-ms final drain and accounted codec tail; no audio work on video callbacks/GPU worker; no video CPU readback; normal audio period; at most two added threads; at most 8 MiB application-owned audio storage and 500 ms queued PCM; finite cancelled transport and bounded retry/retention. Audio-device availability never gates video admission/readiness, media identity, intentional stop or publication of an otherwise valid video. Optional transport setup falls through to video-only within the existing startup deadline. Endpoint failures stay upstream of the healthy PCM writer. Managed complete-prefix EOF is supported; arbitrary input corruption/partial-I/O failure can damage the shared mux and carries no unconditional video-survival promise. Mux/video/container integrity remains mandatory.
